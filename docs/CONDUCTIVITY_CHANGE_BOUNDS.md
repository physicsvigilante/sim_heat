# Predicting the effect of replacing one conducting rod

For a steady, connected, two-terminal network, choose a rod and multiply its conductivity by a positive factor m while keeping its geometry and every other property fixed. The offline observer returns a lower and upper resistance in K/W using only the pinned baseline. Both coincident bath temperatures and reversed physical bath ordering are compatible with a normalized unit terminal excitation.

Let R be baseline terminal resistance and s the selected rod's fraction of the network dissipation under unit terminal current. The sum of all rod fractions is one. The old current distribution still meets all junction balances after replacing the rod. Evaluating its quadratic dissipation gives an upper bound on the minimizing new resistance, R [1 + s (1/m - 1)]. Under fixed unit terminal temperature difference, the old temperature field is also admissible. Its quadratic form gives an upper bound on new conductance, (1/R) [1 + s (m - 1)]. Taking the reciprocal gives the lower resistance bound. The same s appears because normalizing the field scales each contribution and the total equally.

For the composite wall, replacing the insert with a material of 7/3 times its conductivity gives bounds of 1/3 and 11/28 K/W. The fully recomputed revised study gives 3/8 K/W. Material replacement at an exactly balanced bridge crossbar leaves terminal resistance unchanged. Output carries the original source pins and identifies the multiplier as hypothetical. The CLI accepts positive decimal, integer and rational multipliers, with at most 32 digits per part.
